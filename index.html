Learning built
around real work.

Cue finds the skill a team is missing in real work, turns it into an 8 minute practice with an AI avatar, and shows if it moved the numbers.

01 / 04 · Problem

Finishing a course doesn’t always mean getting better.

Most learning platforms are built around a content library. People watch, mark it done, and go back to work.

Where learning can fall short
  • It's far from the work. You learn in one tab and work in another. The two rarely meet.
  • Watching is only the start. A video can show how a tough conversation goes, but without practice, people still freeze in a real one.
  • It's hard to tell if it helped. The report says you finished. It can't say if anything changed.
The opportunity

Kaltura already has video and AI avatars. Cue builds on them so people can practice the hard moments at work, and checks if the practice made a difference.

02 / 04 · Focus

I chose sales as an example, because its calls are often recorded.

Every deal lives in the CRM too, a large database to learn from. So it’s easy to see where a team gets stuck, and later, whether it got better.

Who we design for
  • Noa Levin, sales team leadShow me where my team loses, and help me fix it fast.
  • Sam Rivera, account executiveHelp me nail Thursday's call without losing my week.
  • Ira Klein, head of enablementShow me which practice moves the numbers, so I can scale it.
03 / 04 · Solution

How a gap becomes a skill.

A manager spots a gap, Cue builds a practice, the team rehearses with an avatar, and it shows in the way they work.

  1. 1 SpotNoa sees where her team gets stuck, and why.
  2. 2 BuildCue drafts a practice. Noa edits it and sends it.
  3. 3 PracticeSam rehearses with an AI avatar before the real conversation.
  4. 4 CoachNoa sees who is ready, and who needs a 1:1.
  5. 5 ProveIra sees what worked and shares it with every team.
04 / 04 · Success

How I'd know it works.

Three signals that the experience works, for managers and for their teams.

  1. Managers act fast

    A gap becomes an assigned practice in minutes, not a project.

    MetricTime from a spotted gap to a sent practice
  2. People practice in time

    The practice reaches people where they already work, and it's short enough to finish before the real moment.

    Metric% who finish before their real conversation
  3. People come back

    They do another round because it helps, not because they were told to.

    Metric% who do a second round without being asked
